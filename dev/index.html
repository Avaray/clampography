Masters of Stealth and Silence

Cats (Felis catus) have co-habited with humans for roughly 10,000 years, yet they remain one of the most misunderstood animals on Earth. Unlike dogs, cats were never truly domesticated — they chose us. You can read more about this feline self-selection in the archaeological record from the Fertile Crescent, where wolves wildcats first began lurking around grain stores. Scholars from the UC system have documented over 40 distinct vocalisations that domestic cats use exclusively when communicating with their human companions.

A cat’s hearing range spans 48 Hz–85 kHz. Press Ctrl + Q to remind yourself that a cat can hear frequencies 1.6 octaves higher than any human. Their ears rotate up to 180° independently — acting like biological satellite dishes pointed directly at the sound of a treat bag opening three rooms away. The technical term for this skill is pinnae rotation, derived from the Latin pinna (feather, fin).

The Physics of the Righting Reflex

Cats have a specialised collarbone that lets them always land on their feet during a fall — a phenomenon called the aerial righting reflex. They also have no clavicle attachment to their spine, allowing them to squeeze through any gap their head fits through. This is sometimes called the liquid cat hypothesis, and while it’s not a real scientific paper, it should be.

The minimum safe height for the righting reflex to complete is approximately 30 cm. Terminal velocity for a cat is roughly 97 km/h, achieved at around 5 storeys. Below that, survival rate is paradoxically lower because the cat hasn’t had time to relax. This is documented in the JAVMA and confirmed by every cat who has ever stared at you from a high shelf with complete contempt.

Sacred Animals of Ancient Egypt

In ancient Egypt, cats were considered divine. The penalties for harming one were severe, the worship was genuine, and the merchandise was excellent.

  • The goddess Bastet had the head of a cat and was worshipped at Bubastis
  • Killing a cat, even accidentally, carried severe punishment — sometimes fatal:

    According to Diodorus Siculus, Roman soldiers who accidentally killed cats were sometimes lynched by mobs, regardless of diplomatic immunity.

  • Cats were mummified and buried alongside their owners — often with mummified mice for the afterlife journey
  • Estimated mummified cats found in Egypt: over 300,000, discovered in 1888 alone

  • The city of Per-Bast was literally named after Bastet. Archaeologists have found:

    • Amulets — mass-produced in bronze, faience, and gold
    • Votive statues — ranging from 2 cm to 60 cm tall
    • Temple complexes described by Herodotus as “the most beautiful in all Egypt”

Cats spend 70% of their lives sleeping — roughly 13–16 hours a day. During light sleep, their ears still rotate to monitor sounds. In deep REM sleep, paws twitch from vivid dreams — likely involving birds they’ve never caught and keyboards they have.

Purring: 25–150 Hz Healing Frequencies

The purr frequency of 25–50 Hz has been shown to promote bone density and wound healing in humans. Which means your cat is either a therapeutic device or just pretending to be one in exchange for food. The technical jury is still out. The cat, meanwhile, has already decided.

  1. Slow blinking — recognised sign of trust. Return the blink. Here’s how.
  2. Head-bunting your forehead — marks you as owned territory
  3. Cat yawning in your presence — signals they feel unthreatened. Or bored. Probably both.
  4. Chirping at birds — instinctive jaw-chattering reflex, possibly from frustration
  5. Kneading (“making biscuits”) — comfort behaviour from kittenhood, now deployed on your laptop stomach at 3am
  6. Bringing you dead prey — a remedial lesson in hunting. Be humble. Read the research.

Vibrissae
The technical term for a cat’s whiskers, used for navigation and sensing in low-light conditions. They span exactly the width of the cat’s body — a built-in max-width detector.
Clowder
A group of adult cats. Also known as a glaring when they are unfamiliar with each other. Also known as a Monday when they’re yours.
Kindle
A group of kittens born to one mother. Not to be confused with the electronic reader, though both drain your battery.
Felid
A member of the family Felidae, which includes all cats from the Felis catus on your sofa to Panthera leo on the savanna. All equally convinced they are superior to you.
Nepetalactone [1]
The active compound in catnip (Nepeta cataria) that binds to feline olfactory receptors. Affects approximately 50–70% of cats. The remaining 30–50% are simply too dignified.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why do cats bring you dead animals?

Your cat considers you a terrible hunter. By bringing you prey, it is attempting to teach you to survive. This is not a gift — it is a remedial lesson in basic predator skills. Several studies support this interpretation. Be grateful. Be humble.

Can cats get high on catnip?

Not exactly. Nepetalactone, the active compound in catnip, binds to feline olfactory receptors and mimics a cat pheromone. The reaction — rolling, drooling, vocalising — lasts about 10 minutes. Roughly 30% of cats carry a recessive gene that makes them completely unresponsive. They judge the other 70% silently from across the room.

Do cats dream?

Yes. Cats experience REM sleep, during which their brains show activity patterns similar to humans. The twitching paws you see are likely responses to a dream-hunt. Research published in Science suggests cats replay the events of their day during sleep — which, for most cats, consists entirely of filing tax returns staring out of windows.

Is it true cats have a dominant paw?

Yes. Studies show that roughly 40% of cats are right-pawed, 40% left-pawed, and 20% ambidextrous — a higher ambidexterity rate than humans. Female cats tend to be right-pawed; male cats tend to be left-pawed. No one has asked any cats for comment.

Why does my cat knock things off tables?

There are three leading theories: (1) they are testing gravity to make sure it still works; (2) they are checking if the object is alive; (3) they simply enjoy the sound of your patience ceramic mug shattering. Current scientific consensus favours option (3). See: Newton’s Third Law of Cat Physics.
