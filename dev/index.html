Masters of Stealth and Silence

Cats (Felis catus) have co-habited with humans for roughly 10,000 years, yet they remain one of the most misunderstood animals on Earth. Unlike dogs, cats were never truly domesticated — they chose us. You can read more about this feline self-selection in the archaeological record from the Fertile Crescent, where wolves wildcats first began lurking around grain stores.

A cat’s hearing range spans 48 Hz–85 kHz. Press Q to remind yourself that a cat can hear frequencies 1.6 octaves higher than any human. Their ears rotate up to 180° independently, acting like biological satellite dishes.

The Physics of the Righting Reflex

Cats have a specialised collarbone that lets them always land on their feet during a fall — a phenomenon called aerial righting reflex. They also have no clavicle attachment to their spine, allowing them to squeeze through any gap their head fits through.

Sacred Animals of Ancient Egypt

In ancient Egypt, cats were considered divine. Key facts about their revered status:

  • The goddess Bastet had the head of a cat
  • Killing a cat, even accidentally, carried severe punishment:

    According to Diodorus Siculus, Roman soldiers who accidentally killed cats were sometimes lynched by mobs, regardless of diplomatic immunity.

  • Cats were mummified and buried alongside their owners
  • Estimated mummified cats found in Egypt: over 300,000

Feline Biology and Behaviour

Whisker Mechanics and Spatial Awareness

Cats spend 70% of their lives sleeping — roughly 13–16 hours a day. During light sleep, their ears still rotate to monitor sounds and whiskers twitch in response to stimuli. In deep REM sleep, paws twitch from vivid dreams.

  • Cats cannot taste sweetness — they lack the Tas1r2 gene
    1. This is a recessive mutation shared by all felids
    2. Lions and tigers share this same trait
      • Even big cats are indifferent to sugar
      • They primarily rely on scent and texture
    3. Evolutionary biologists believe this is why they are obligate carnivores

  • A group of cats is called a clowder. A group of kittens is called a kindle.

    These are real words — though they do sound suspiciously whimsical.

  • Cats rarely meow at other cats — meowing is a language invented just for humans

The language of cat body signals, in order of social importance:

  1. Slow blinking — recognised sign of trust and safety
  2. Head-bunting your forehead — marks you as owned territory
  3. Cat yawning in your presence — signals they feel unthreatened
  4. Chirping at birds — instinctive jaw-chattering reflex
  5. Kneading (“making biscuits”) — comfort behaviour from kittenhood
  6. Bringing you dead prey — a remedial lesson in hunting. Be humble.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why do cats bring you dead animals?

Your cat considers you a terrible hunter. By bringing you prey, it is attempting to teach you to survive. This is not a gift — it is a remedial lesson in basic predator skills. Be grateful. Be humble.

Can cats get high on catnip?

Not exactly. Nepetalactone, the active compound in catnip, binds to feline olfactory receptors and mimics a cat pheromone. The reaction — rolling, drooling, vocalising — lasts about 10 minutes. Roughly 30% of cats carry a recessive gene that makes them completely unresponsive to it.

Do cats dream?

Yes. Cats experience REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep, during which their brains show activity patterns similar to humans. The twitching paws and whiskers you see are likely responses to a dream-hunt in progress.
